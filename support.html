Getting the most out of MuteBar

The menu bar button disappeared

macOS hides menu bar items when there is no room. Pull the menu bar with a two-finger swipe on a trackpad, or hold ⌘ and drag the button to reposition it. Some menu bar managers can also show hidden items.

An app doesn't appear in the list

MuteBar lists apps that currently have an active audio output. Start playing sound in the app and it will appear within a couple of seconds. Helper processes are grouped under the parent app where possible, so Chrome and Electron apps show up as one entry.

An app is muted but I still hear it

Some apps play sound from a separate process that isn't registered with Core Audio until it has played before. Open and close the app, then try again. If it persists, quit and relaunch MuteBar from the menu.

Checking what MuteBar is doing

Choose Open Log in the MuteBar menu to see a local diagnostic log. It records which apps were muted and when, never audio.

Running from the command line

MuteBar ships with two diagnostics:

  • MuteBar --list — lists every audio process and how it is grouped.
  • MuteBar --mute-test <pid> 10 — holds a mute tap on a process ID for ten seconds.
